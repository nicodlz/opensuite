Principles

Own it. Really.

Esuno is not a service you subscribe to. It is software you install, on a server you control, wired to providers you pick.

  • 01

    No landlord

    No central Esuno service and no account with us. Every installation stands on its own.

  • 02

    Your providers

    Pick the S3-compatible storage you trust and use your own provider accounts and credentials.

  • 03

    Standards, not silos

    SMTP, IMAP, JMAP, CalDAV and CardDAV, built in. Your data speaks open protocols.

  • 04

    Encrypted backups

    Encrypted, scheduled backups with documented restore procedures you can rehearse.

  • 05

    Leave anytime

    Your data stays in open formats and standard protocols. Moving out is a procedure, not a negotiation.

  • 06

    Free software

    Licensed AGPL-3.0-or-later. Read the source, change it, run it, share it.

Mail first

Mail is the one thing that cannot wait.

Incoming mail is received by a dedicated relay, kept in durable storage and recorded before it reaches your mailbox. If your server is briefly away, messages wait and are replayed when it returns. The web app and Drive are never in that path.

  1. Sender Anywhere on the internet
  2. Inbound relay Accepts the message for you
  3. Durable buffer Keeps a copy until delivery
  4. Ingest ledger Records, delivers, replays
  5. Your mailbox Stalwart, on your server

Self-host

One small server. One Compose file.

Esuno starts on a single small VPS. A step-by-step runbook covers DNS, secrets, storage, backups and verification before you switch your mail over.

  • An Ubuntu VPS with Docker Compose
  • A domain you control
  • An S3-compatible bucket
  • An Amazon SES account for sending and receiving
Read the production runbook

Scale

Start on one server. Grow without a ceiling.

The same Esuno that fits on a small VPS is built to grow with you: for a family, a company or many more. Storage, mail and the web workspace each scale on their own, so you add capacity exactly where you need it.

  1. Day one

    One small server

    Mail, calendar, files and passwords in a single Compose file, on a modest VPS.

  2. Growing

    Storage that keeps up

    Files and mail contents can live in S3-compatible object storage, and encrypted backups go there too. Grow it as far as you like.

  3. At scale

    As many machines as you need

    Independent services, and a mail engine that supports clustering on shared storage. Add servers when you grow, keep one when you don't.
